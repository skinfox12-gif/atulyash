The everyday compromises

Everyday atta may carry compromises you cannot see.

01

The First Compromise: Wheat Selection

It begins with the wheat.

Wheat quality can vary widely. When different lots or varieties are blended, consistency of the final atta can change.

The Atulyash Way

We begin with carefully selected, single-origin MP Sharbati wheat. No blending with other wheat varieties.

02

The Second Compromise: Milling Stone

Stone-ground does not always mean natural stone

Most mills, including most neighbourhood chakkis, use man-made synthetic emery stones, manufactured by bonding abrasive mineral particles into hard grinding discs. Large-scale systems may use steel rollers.

The Atulyash Way

We use natural stone chakkis, no emery-stone grinding, no industrial steel rollers.

03

The Third Compromise: Milling Speed

Speed increases output. But it also increases heat.

Speed creates friction. Friction creates heat. And heat can affect the grain’s natural oils, aroma and character.

The Atulyash Way

Atulyash mills slowly to keep the process gentle and controlled.

04

The Fourth Compromise: Chokar

Whole wheat deserves its natural chokar.

In many milling systems, the wheat grain is separated in stages. Bran/chokar may be removed to make atta appear smoother, improve shelf stability, or be sold separately for other food and commercial uses.

The Atulyash Way

We retain the natural chokar. Nothing removed. Nothing added. Atta in its purest form.

05

The Fifth Compromise: Discipline After Milling

What happens after milling matters too.

After milling, handling, exposure and packing can all influence the cleanliness and integrity of the final product.

The Atulyash Way

Our post-milling process is structured to minimise unnecessary human contact, with controlled handling and automated packing in a clean environment.

06

The Sixth Compromise: Shelf Life

Freshness should not be an afterthought.

Once wheat is milled, its natural oils begin interacting with air. Over time, slow oxidation gradually changes the atta’s aroma and character. Yet most atta is made to travel, sit and stay stable for weeks or months.

The Atulyash Way

We mill in smaller batches and deliver weekly, closer to consumption. No unnecessary long storage.
